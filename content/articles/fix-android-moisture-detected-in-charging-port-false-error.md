## Method 1: Physical Inspection and Gentle Cleaning

False moisture warnings are frequently triggered by microscopic debris or lint within the charging port, mimicking moisture. A thorough, gentle cleaning can often resolve this.

1.  **Power off your device.** This prevents accidental short circuits or damage during cleaning.
2.  **Inspect the charging port.** Use a flashlight to carefully examine the port for any visible lint, dust, corrosion, or foreign objects.
3.  **Gently clean the port.** Use a can of compressed air (held upright and in short bursts) to blow out any debris. Alternatively, use a soft, non-metallic tool like a wooden toothpick or a soft-bristled, anti-static brush to carefully dislodge any stubborn particles. Avoid using metal objects or excessive force.
4.  **Allow to air dry.** Even if no moisture was present, let the device sit for 15-30 minutes after cleaning before attempting to charge.

## Method 2: Clear USB Settings Cache and Data

Software glitches within the Android System's USB handling processes can cause persistent false warnings. Clearing the cache and data for these system apps can reset their state.

1.  Navigate to **Settings > Apps**.
2.  Tap the **filter icon** (often a downward arrow or three horizontal lines) or the **three vertical dots** in the upper right corner, then select **Show system apps** (or **Show system apps** toggle).
3.  Scroll down and locate **Android System** or **USB Settings** (the exact name may vary slightly by device and One UI version).
4.  Tap on the identified app, then select **Storage**.
5.  Tap **Clear cache**, then tap **Clear data** (confirm if prompted).
6.  Go back to the previous screen and tap **Force stop** (confirm if prompted).
7.  **Restart your device** normally. After reboot, attempt to charge.

## Method 3: Soft Reset and Safe Mode Diagnosis

A soft reset can resolve temporary software conflicts or sensor miscalibrations. If the issue persists, booting into Safe Mode can help determine if a third-party application is interfering.

1.  **Perform a Soft Reset:** Press and hold the **Power button** and **Volume Down button** simultaneously for 7-10 seconds until your device restarts. This simulates a battery pull and can clear minor system errors without erasing data.
2.  **Test in Safe Mode:**
    *   **Power off your device** completely.
    *   Press and hold the **Power button**.
    *   When the Samsung logo (or Android logo) appears, release the **Power button** and immediately press and hold the **Volume Down button**.
    *   Continue holding **Volume Down** until the device fully boots and you see "Safe mode" displayed in the bottom-left corner of the screen.
    *   While in Safe Mode, attempt to charge your device. If the warning disappears and charging works, a recently installed third-party app is likely causing the conflict. Restart your device normally to exit Safe Mode and uninstall recently added apps one by one to identify the culprit.

## Method 4: Factory Data Reset (Last Resort)

If all other troubleshooting steps fail, a factory data reset can resolve deep-seated software issues by restoring your device to its original factory state. This should be considered a last resort as it erases all user data.

1.  **Back up all important data.** This includes photos, videos, contacts, and app data. Use Samsung Cloud, Google Drive, or a PC transfer.
2.  Navigate to **Settings > General management > Reset > Factory data reset**.
3.  Review the information, then tap **Reset device**.
4.  Enter your PIN or password if prompted.
5.  Tap **Delete all** to confirm. Your device will restart and begin the reset process. Once complete, set up your device as new and test the charging functionality before restoring your backup.
